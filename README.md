To produce a DEM with a prescribed accuracy, the producer can either select the cell size for a given measuring instrument or determine the instrument accuracy required for a given cell size. Establishing an analytical relationship between these quantities facilitates this trade-off. Existing approaches are empirical, although they have generally been validated experimentally. In this paper, we derive such a relationship analytically for the conventional measure of elevation accuracy based on the mean squared error (MSE), revealing some limitations of the empirical approaches. We prove that the elevation MSE is bounded by a polynomial in the cell size, h. For a given interpolation method, the exponents of the polynomial are fixed, whereas its coefficients depend on local terrain characteristics at the control points. These coefficients are nevertheless computable, and here we show how.
